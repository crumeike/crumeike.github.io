About Me

I am an AI researcher whose work spans multimodal vision-language models, scientific imaging, computer vision, and the development of intelligent systems that support real-world decision-making. My research focuses on building models that enhance understanding in science, engineering, and community resilience. I work at the intersection of data-driven learning and practical applications, creating methods that are both technically rigorous and meaningful to society.

I have conducted research at Argonne National Laboratory through two appointments in 2024 and 2025, where I contributed to projects involving multimodal models for scientific discovery and biomedical imaging. My work has also addressed post-disaster building damage assessment, urban resilience, sensor-driven flood monitoring, and AI approaches for low-data scientific environments.

Selected Projects

Explore detailed project descriptions and code repositories on GitHub

Argonne National Laboratory (2025)

Domain-adapted vision-language models for scientific and biomedical imaging. Work includes adapting general-purpose foundation models to low-data scientific domains, designing vision-language systems for X-ray ptychography and radiation imaging, applying reinforcement learning (GRPO) to autonomous scientific workflows, and scaling training across HPC and multi-GPU environments at Argonne National Laboratory. Developed PtychoBench benchmark for evaluating foundation model adaptation strategies.

VLMs Qwen-2.5 Llama-3.2 GRPO HPC

The University of Alabama (2023-Present)

End-to-end computer vision systems for rapid building damage estimation following natural disasters. Includes damage detection and classification using YOLO, SSD, and RT-DETR architectures for tornado and windstorm events. Deployed system processing 333 images per second, reducing evaluation time from weeks to minutes. Integrated imagery with geospatial data and developed multimodal deep learning pipelines. Collaborative work with NIST Disaster and Failure Studies Program.

YOLO RT-DETR Computer Vision NIST

Argonne National Laboratory (2024)

Vision-language and multimodal models for biomedical image interpretation and low-dose imaging applications. Built comprehensive data curation pipeline processing 42,673 scientific articles, generating 171,000+ captions and 165,000+ images. Fine-tuned LLaVA models on 50,882 image-text pairs, achieving 135% performance improvement and 96.6% hallucination reduction over baselines. Designed AI systems that support clinical and scientific workflows for low-dose radiation therapy imaging.

LLaVA LoRA Medical AI Argonne Lab

The University of Alabama (2022-2023)

Intelligent sensing and decision-support tools for urban infrastructure resilience. Deployed IoT flood monitoring networks with edge computing for real-time environmental sensing, processing over 25,000 data points across 7-week field studies at University of Alabama campus. Developed city-scale resilience modeling frameworks and integrated standards-based (OIIE) data architectures for monitoring civil infrastructure systems. Earned two first-place innovation awards.

IoT Edge Computing OIIE Standards Urban Systems
